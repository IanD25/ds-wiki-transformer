## Methodological reflections

**1. The tool surfaced GT04 ↔ IT08 correctly as "high similarity, unlinked, cross-type."** That's exactly the surface of vocabulary-overlap drift. Without the deep research having been done, this would have looked like an "obvious link to add." With the deep research having been done, we know it's a category error and the right action is no link, plus the disambiguation that just landed. **The hypothesis generator is not a link-recommender; it's a candidate-surfacer that requires charter-discipline triage before action.**

**2. The hypothesis generator's `surprise_factor` is type-pair-relative.** It's a Bayesian-flavored surprise measure: "this pair is N× more similar than the average pair of (type_A, type_B)." Within a single textbook cluster like EM or FL, all pairs are highly similar, so the baseline is high and the surprise factor is small. The very high surprise factors we see (1.25–1.37×) come from pairs where the *types* don't usually live near each other in embedding space, OR where one entry is in a small cluster (FL, OP) where the baseline is dragged down by cross-cluster reference_law pairs. Both readings are valid; both produce candidates that require human triage.

**3. None of the 25 highest-surprise unlinked pairs is a genuine "novel cross-domain bridge"** that wasn't already in some form in the wiki structure. The most interesting finding is the GT04↔IT08 conflation; the rest are routine link-cleanup or vocabulary-overlap-flag candidates. This is consistent with the M0 audit's observation that the wiki has a lot of internal structure but the connections are mostly to established adjacent science, not novel bridges.
