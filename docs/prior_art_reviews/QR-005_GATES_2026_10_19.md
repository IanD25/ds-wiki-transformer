## Part 1 — Is QR-005 well-posed?

**As logged:** *Will tabletop experiments show gravity carrying quantum information (mediating entanglement), or the decoherence and force noise that entropic and thermodynamic models predict?*

**No.** Four problems:

1. **The two options are not rivals.** Thermodynamic derivations of Einstein's equation (Jacobson 1995, 2016; Padmanabhan; Verlinde 2011's field-equation reading) predict nothing different in the lab. They all use ħ and are silent on whether the field is quantized. Only specific microscopic models make tabletop predictions: Carney et al. 2025's free-energy models; Wang & Taylor's entropic springs. Carney, Stamp & Taylor 2019 say the predictions of emergent models "are unclear". The wording itself shows the drift pattern **`emergent-read-as-classical`**: it treats "thermodynamic" as the alternative to "quantum".
2. **The real dividing line is entangling vs non-entangling (noisy) gravity.** The rivals to quantum gravity are classical, stochastic or collapse models: GT13–GT16, Carney's local model, and others.
3. **The two options are not exhaustive.**
   - Diósi–Penrose collapse adds noise *and* can entangle at short range (Trillo & Navascués 2025).
   - Carney's non-local model can entangle.
   - Aziz & Howl 2025 claim classical gravity entangles quantum-field matter (contested).
4. **"Will experiments show" is a forecast, not a question science can answer now.**

### The re-scoped question

> **QR-005b.** Does the gravitational interaction between two masses create entanglement? Equivalently, within stated model classes (Galilean-invariant, time-local, Newtonian on average; Fabiano et al. 2026, preprint): are gravitating masses quieter than the minimum noise any non-entangling model must add?

**Plain language (charter rule 5):** *Can gravity link two objects quantum mechanically, or must it, like any classical go-between, add a minimum amount of jitter?*

The sub-questions S1–S7 from the map are gated individually in Part 4.

## Part 3 — Gate 2: worth asking

Gate 2 runs on QR-005b, which is not `answered`. The thermodynamic half and the irreversibility angle are folded at Gate 1.

**1. Triviality.** No. Whether gravity entangles is not derivable from known results. What *is* derivable is that LOCC cannot entangle, so a positive witness excludes LOCC gravity. That is a textbook theorem, already recorded in GT12.

**2. Absence.** There is no absence of the *question*: it is an active, named programme. What is absent is a *result*, and that is explained:

| | Explanation | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| a | Wrong vocabulary | No. Searched in the field's terms: GIE, BMV, LOCC, classical-quantum gravity, collapse. |
| b | Done but not as a paper | No. |
| c | Too obvious to publish | No. |
| d | False premise | Only in the original wording (Part 1); the re-scoped form is sound. |
| e | **Experts avoid it for a known reason** | **Not avoided, but blocked experimentally.** The direct test is ~7.5 orders of magnitude short in superposed mass and ~10 in measured source mass, and the noise route is ~9 orders short of the threshold (map §C; GT12, GT17). Plávala 2026 places even optimistic projections "outside of short-term future" for the direct experiment. |
| f | **Data or tools only recently available** | **Partly.** Plávala 2026 argues that today's matter-wave interferometers suffice for an *indirect* proof, under one of two stated assumptions. Lami et al. 2024 propose a test with no delocalisation (torsion pendula). Both open routes that are cheaper than BMV, but neither has been carried out as a decisive test. |
| g | Genuinely unasked | No: it is asked by a whole community. |

**3. Obvious or erroneous?** The re-scoped form is well-posed, with two caveats.
- A positive witness rules out LOCC gravity, but not by itself classical non-local models. It proves gravitons only with extra assumptions (S3; Fragkos et al. 2022; Martín-Martínez & Perche 2023).
- The "equivalently, below the noise floor" clause holds only within Fabiano et al.'s model class, and that rests on one group's preprint.

**4. The expert view.** The likely reply from a researcher in the field: *"It's the right question, and worth the experiments; the decisive test is far off, and meanwhile classical alternatives are being squeezed by noise and collapse bounds. What a positive result would mean is still argued."* Sourced:
- the experimental gap and timeline: Plávala 2026, read;
- the squeeze: Oppenheim et al. 2023, Janse et al. 2024 and Donadi et al. 2021, read;
- the interpretation debate: Bose et al. 2025 *RMP* passages and Fragkos et al. 2022, read;
- the bottleneck being theory: Plávala 2026, conclusions, read.

**5. Should it be asked here?**
- **What each answer forbids.**
  - *Yes, entangling:* rules out every LOCC account of gravity, including Oppenheim's classical–quantum gravity, mean-field gravity and Carney's local model.
  - *No, quiet below the floor while not entangling:* impossible within the model class, so a quiet measurement *is* a yes.
  - *Noisy and not entangling:* rules out perturbative quantum gravity at low energy.
  - So the question is not decorative: either answer forbids a lot.
- **Decidable with data and compute this project can obtain?** **No.** Every route needs new experiments by specialist groups. The project has no data, apparatus or theory lever the field lacks. The cross-domain view adds nothing decisive here: the owner's thermodynamic angle is already the published irreversibility theorem.
- **Cost:** monitoring is cheap. Research effort would be wasted.

**Gate 2 verdict on QR-005b: DROP as an in-house research question** — tag `undecidable-with-obtainable-data`. **Keep it in the log as a monitored open-in-literature question.**
- **Recommendation:** the owner parks it with a resume condition.
- **Resume when any of:**
  - a published gravity-mediated entanglement result;
  - a below-threshold gravitational noise measurement;
  - Plávala's single-mass verification being performed and the indirect inference being accepted or rebutted in print;
  - peer-reviewed publication or rebuttal of Fabiano et al. 2026;
  - a peer-reviewed resolution of Aziz & Howl 2025.

## Recommendation (for the owner)

- **The thermodynamic half of QR-005 is answered, and the answer is "they don't compete".** It is folded.
- **Your instinct that gravity "is thermodynamic", made precise, is a theorem others proved:** if gravity is classical, its coupling to quantum matter must be irreversible, and therefore noisy (Galley, Giacomini & Selby 2023). That is a good result to know, and it is already the basis of the noise tests (GT14, GT17).
- **The real question, "does gravity entangle?", is one of the field's open problems.** This project cannot decide it. Park it as a watch item with the resume conditions above.
