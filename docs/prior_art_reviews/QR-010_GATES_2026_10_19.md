## Part 1 — Is QR-010 well-posed?

**As logged:** *What lets the largest machine-learning models escape the complexity tax, where do they still pay it, and how is the field developing these themes?*

**Mostly yes, with three problems:**
1. **The premise is half false.** Measured on *effective* rather than raw complexity, modern machine learning does not escape the tax. It is an extreme case of QR-009's cures (map §A, §E): more independent evidence per effective parameter, pooling, and shrinkage toward simple solutions. The "escape" exists only against the raw-parameter-count meter that classical statistics itself never endorsed (QR-009 §B2). That is the drift pattern **`restatement-in-new-vocabulary`** in mild form: the question's own term, "complexity tax", is not a technical term (QR-009 §A).
2. **"Complexity" names several objects.** QR-009's vocabulary warning applies. The map adds four "effective" quantities that must not be stacked (drift note, map §F).
3. **"How is the field developing" is a survey request, not a research question.** It is answered by the map, not by a gate.

### The re-scoped question

> **QR-010 (as gated).** Measured on effective rather than raw complexity, under what conditions does a heavily over-parameterised learner predict well out of sample, and through which mechanisms does it still lose accuracy?

**Plain language (charter rule 5):** *Why do huge AI models predict well despite having more knobs than data points, and where does the old overfitting penalty still apply?*

## Part 4 — S7: the MacKay–Fisher link, and the ML2 → IT09 decision

**The question (map §I, S7).** Do the sloppy-models papers already connect MacKay's effective number of parameters to the Fisher-information spectrum? If so, ML2 (implicit regularisation and effective complexity) could be linked to IT09 (sloppy models, Machta et al. 2013) with a source.

**What was read** (table at the top):
- **MacKay 1992:** defines γ = Σ_a λ_a/(λ_a + α) over the eigenvalues of βB. In his linear-Gaussian setting, βB is the Hessian of the data misfit, which is the Fisher information of the weights; he does not use that name. Directions with λ_a ≫ α are "well determined" (γ_a ≈ 1); those with λ_a ≪ α are fixed by the prior (γ_a ≈ 0).
- **Transtrum et al. 2015 and Sethna's 2024 book draft:** describe the same structure qualitatively. A few stiff Fisher or Hessian directions are "well determined", the rest sloppy, giving "a type of low effective dimensionality". **Neither cites MacKay or uses a γ-type count.**
- **Quinn et al. 2023:** connects sloppiness to AIC/BIC, Jeffreys and optimal priors, and minimum description length. **It does not cite MacKay either.**
- **Maddox et al. 2020:** carry MacKay's count into deep learning, on the training-loss Hessian, with "preliminary" Fisher-matrix experiments. **They do not cite the sloppy-models literature.**
- **A targeted web search** (sloppy models × Occam factor or effective number of parameters) found nothing joining the two.

**Answer: no.** The two literatures describe the same object, the eigenvalue spectrum of the Hessian or Fisher information measured against a noise or prior scale, but neither connects itself to the other in anything read here. The bridge is a one-line identity: for Gaussian noise, the data-misfit Hessian is the Fisher information. So MacKay's γ is a soft count of Fisher eigenvalues above the prior precision. That is a `trivial-from-known-result`, not a finding.

**Decision, per the owner's rule ("if the link is supported and sourced"):** the link is mathematically supported but not sourced as a connection, so **no ML2 → IT09 link is made.** ML2's cross-reference text says so and points here. If the owner later wants the link, the honest form is `couples to` (shared state variable: the Hessian/Fisher eigenvalue spectrum), justified by the identity above and citing MacKay 1992 and Transtrum et al. 2015 separately.

**What S7 does not license.** Nothing here ties ML2, IT09 or MacKay's γ to the wiki's retired D_eff object (the participation ratio of a Fisher spectrum, PR_FIM). γ is a different function of the spectrum (a soft threshold count, not a participation ratio). It is computed for fitted models with a prior, not for graphs. The QR-009 and QR-010 drift notes stand.
